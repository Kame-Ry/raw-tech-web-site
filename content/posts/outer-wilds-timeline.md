*Outer Wilds* is the only game I've finished and immediately wished I could forget, so I could play it again. It's also a game that gives you its story in fragments: a wall of Nomai text here, a recording there, a dead end you only understand three loops later. It never once assembles those fragments for you. That's the point. It's also the reason that, once you're done, you want to see the whole thing laid out flat.

So I built that: **[The Outer Wilds Timeline](/outer-wilds/timeline.html)**.

> **This is a spoiler page.** It covers both *Outer Wilds* (2019) and *Echoes of the Eye* (2021), ending included. The page opens behind a gate that asks you to confirm you've finished before it shows you anything. Please don't click through it if you haven't.

---

## What's on it

**Six eras, 68 events.** The record runs from before the universe existed (the Eye is older than everything else in the story, including time) through the Strangers, the Nomai, the 281,000 years of quiet in between, the loop itself, and what happens at the Eye. Every entry is filterable by era, by people (Strangers, Nomai, Hearthians, cosmic events), and by location, plus a search box over the whole record.

**A working orrery.** The centrepiece is a simulated model of the solar system on a 22-minute clock. You can play it, scrub it, jump to the moments that matter, watch the sand drain off Ash Twin and bury the Sunless City, reveal the Stranger, and set off the supernova. Hovering a body tells you what's happening on it at that instant in the loop.

**A codex.** Four peoples, two of them extinct long before you were born, plus the things living in the gaps between them.

**The final fork.** A diagram of the three pieces of knowledge that actually end the loop, and the two optional threads that change who's sitting around the campfire when it does. Nothing in *Outer Wilds* is gated by an item or an upgrade, because every lock is something you know, and I wanted a page that made that structure visible.

---

## Notes

It's a single self-contained HTML page: no frameworks, no external assets, no tracking. The artwork is original vector illustration rather than ripped game assets, and it follows your system light/dark preference, though deep space is very much the intended mood. There's an optional ambient track you can leave off.

It's a fan-made study guide, not anything official. *Outer Wilds* belongs to Mobius Digital. The dates before the present day are the ones the Nomai and the Hearthians recorded themselves, which is to say: approximate.
